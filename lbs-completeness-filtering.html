Bias & Data Quality in GPS Mobility Data

  1. Home
  2. LBS Completeness Filtering

Project information

  • Category: PhD Research, Civil Engineering — University of Michigan
  • Lab: Infrastructure for All (INFRALL) Lab
  • Status: Accepted for presentation, TRB 2026 & ASCE ICTD 2026
  • Data: Multi-year national smartphone GPS panel data; Met Council parcel data; American Community Survey demographics

The Problem

Raw smartphone location data has to be filtered before it's usable — but the thresholds that decide who makes the cut are set by convention, not evidence. With no ground truth to check them against, it's unclear whether they're removing noise or quietly removing entire populations.

Approach

I built a classifier to predict when a device's inferred home location is unreliable, using proxy labels in place of ground truth — turning a guess into an evidence-based cutoff. Then, instead of checking representativeness at one fixed threshold, I tracked how the retained sample's demographics shift as filtering tightens, making the equity tradeoff visible instead of hidden.

Key Results

  • Replaced arbitrary filtering conventions with a data-driven classifier
  • Validated across two years of data to confirm results hold over time
  • Checked across different seasonal windows to rule out artifacts
  • Showed representativeness shifts continuously with filtering — not just at one cutoff

Why It Matters

This is a data-quality problem with no ground truth to check against — the same challenge that shows up anywhere a pipeline has to filter noisy, passively-collected data and decide who gets kept.
